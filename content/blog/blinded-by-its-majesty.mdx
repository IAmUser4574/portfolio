## An Awkward Position

A cliffhanger ending. A rushed development. New online technology. 

Sandwiched between *Halo: Combat Evolved*, 
the classic that defined modern game consoles and shooters, and *Halo 3*, the beloved trilogy conclusion so popular
its poster is [still fading on many Gamestop storefront walls](https://www.reddit.com/r/GameStop/comments/1ecz1za/can_anyone_tell_me_why_some_gamestops_still_have/) 
nearly twenty years later,
*Halo 2* is an awkward child saddled with a controversial cliffhanger ending and forgotten lore.



So, why is *Halo 2* actually the best?

## An Inspired Script

In many shooters, even in other Halo games, scriptwriting serves as a thin veneer over the action, loosely justifying the movement between one action set piece and the next. Not Halo 2.

[The opening scenes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8F7OgOCzph4&t=93s) of Halo 2 offer some of the most compelling moments in the franchise 
as they recap Halo CE from both the human and Covenant perspective, neatly staging the key conflicts of the game.

Thel 'Vadam's character introduction is elegant both in story writing and dialogue. The former Covenant batallion leader, disgraced for failing to stop Master Chief, is tried as a 
Heretic, stripped of his name and rank, and branded with a mark of shame. Sentenced to death for his crimes, the Covenant council leaders, the Prophets, decide to temporarily spare
Thel and promote him to the highest combat rank for his race, the Arbiter. He was too useful of an instrument to discard so quickly.

*"What of the council?"* an incredulous Thel asks.

*"The tasks you must undertake as Arbiter are perilous, suicidal. You will die, as each Arbiter has before you. The council shall have their corpse."* Mercy proclaims.

Donning the shining, ancient helmet, Thel retorts, *"What would you have your Arbiter do?"*

Of course, a character named 'Mercy' delivering this news is a little on-the-nose.


## A Stacked Voice Cast

Dawn Hershey, Halo 2's casting director, stepped up to the plate at a critical moment. The Covenant were expanding into a fully voiced, characterized faction in Halo 2. Players were experiencing Halo through a new second protagonist. 
2004-era character graphics were, to put it nicely, in need of quality voice acting to pick up the slack. And Dawn surely knocked it out of the park acquiring the right talent.

### A Voice to the Enemy

Aside from disparate and brief combat voice lines, the Covenant have *zero* spoken dialogue in *Halo: Combat Evolved*. While Cortana does translate a handful of Covenant radio 
transmissions during key moments in the game, the central antagonist faction is largely left uncharacterized, opaque, and mysterious.

While the stoic and reticent covenant troops of *Halo: CE* served as a terrific teaser to the franchise lore, they would not sustain a sequel. *Halo 2* understood the assignment, leveraging its writing and voice casting
to bring to life the dormant foe.

### Covenant Civil War

Chief and Arbiter find themselves embroiled in a slow-building Covenant civil war that climaxes in the game's later levels when the Chief visits High Charity (pictured above). It is through this critical 
setting and story event that the game offers a better glimpse into the extended lore of *Halo*. 

Built around a Forerunner spaceship, the Covenant holy city High Charity is a massive space station that, like the Covenant itself, was built through war, conquest, and religious fervor at the direction
 of the San'Shyuum race (known as the Prophets).

The coil that wound together the Covenant slowly loosened as the blind ambition of Prophet Truth and the Brutes betrayed the other leaders and factions.

It wasn't just dual wielding - *Halo 2* brought so much more "Halo" to *Halo* gameplay. Locales, weapons, vehicles, races, factions, and abilities were all greatly expanded.

The sandbox rounded out as the game added covenant weapon and vehicle solutions to the much more complete human arsenal (Carbine, Beam Rifle, Spectre all served as answers to the UNSC Battle Rifle, Sniper Rifle, and Warthog). 
Items from Halo CE that players couldn't interact with finally became usable - the Covenant Wraith, Energy Sword, and Fuel Rod Gun.

## Developed Against the Odds
### Shattered into pieces, reassembled in months

Full development of *Halo 2* began quickly after the release of *Halo: CE* in 2001, but progress and direction was far from linear as the team sprinted towards a 2004 release.

The team felt immense pressure to deliver a promising spectacle to E3 2003 audiences [and they delivered](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9i-4foLur0g) - but in the process, they had burned resources on a vertical slice
 that ultimately would prove infeasible to integrate into the real game. Months after the E3 demo, Bungie cut crucial levels and story beats, leaving the original vision 
 decimated and incomplete, and leaving the game's ending as a cliffhanger. 

Originally, *Halo 2* was to be a self-contained story with a third act that saw the Arbiter and Chief teaming up, chasing Truth to Earth (where the Ark was originally to be located), 
and defeating him. Instead, much of that story became *Halo 3* and the rest is history.
